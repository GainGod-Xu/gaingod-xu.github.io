Why World Models for Life Science?

Biological AI is increasingly powerful, but most models remain isolated within a single scale: molecules, proteins, cells, or patients. Living systems, however, are connected across scales. A molecular intervention can alter binding, reshape interaction networks, change cellular states, and ultimately affect phenotype.

The challenge is not only to predict biology, but to understand how interventions propagate through it.

Three Fundamental Questions

Understand

What biological mechanisms govern response to intervention?

Mechanistic representationMolecular recognitionCross-scale structure

Predict

State transitionGeneralizationUncertainty

Intervene

What intervention will drive biology toward a desired state?

Inverse designActive learningExperimental planning

What Makes a Biological World Model Trustworthy?

Interpretability

What mechanism drives the prediction?

Uncertainty

Does the model know when it may be wrong?

Robustness

Does the prediction survive perturbation and measurement noise?

Generalization

Does it transfer to unseen molecules, targets, and biological contexts?

Mechanistic Consistency

Does it obey known physical, chemical, and biological constraints?

Validation

Is it tested against experiments, not only benchmarks?

His research develops trustworthy AI for molecular and systems biology, with a long-term goal of building world models that connect molecular interventions to system-level biological responses.
