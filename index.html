Research Vision

Biology is a world of interventions. A small molecule binds a protein or RNA, reshapes molecular interactions, perturbs biological networks, alters cellular states, and ultimately changes phenotype. My research asks three fundamental questions:

Understand

What mechanisms and representations govern biological responses to intervention?

Predict

Can AI predict how unseen interventions reshape biological states, while knowing when its predictions are uncertain?

Intervene

Can we invert these models to identify interventions that drive biological systems toward desired states?

Across all three questions, I focus on trustworthiness: interpretability, uncertainty, robustness, generalization, and mechanistic consistency. The long-term goal is a trustworthy world model of life—one that connects molecular interventions to system-level biological responses and is consistent with biological mechanisms.

Trustworthy AI

A prediction is only useful if we know why it was made, when it may be wrong, and whether it respects the biology.

Interpretability

Why does the model make this prediction?

Uncertainty

When does the model know that it may be wrong?

Robustness

Does the prediction survive perturbations and measurement noise?

Generalization

Does the learned principle transfer beyond the training distribution?

Mechanistic Consistency

Does the prediction respect physical, chemical, and biological mechanisms?

Methodological foundations include parameter-efficient adaptation and multimodal alignment of foundation models (ACL 2026, ECCV 2026), which make large models reliable and cheap to adapt to scientific data.
